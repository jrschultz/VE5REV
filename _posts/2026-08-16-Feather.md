I had previously built a [QRP EFHW](https://jrschultz.github.io/VE5REV/posts/EFHW-QRP/) but I decided to rebuild it with a dedicated radiator. I tried to copy the design philosophy from the [Chameleon CHA LEFS FEATHER EFHW](https://chameleonantenna.com/products/cha-lefs-feather). 

![Feather](/assets/Feather/feather1.webp)

## The Build

I reused two of the Type 43 cores (Size: .827” (21mm) OD. Core: 5943000601). I wrapped it in a 3:21 autotransformer configuration for a 49:1 ratio with 16awg enamel wire. For these smaller cores, it is advisable to use 3 primary turns. This double stack config should be around 87% efficient. I put the cores on a [3D printed 'sled'](https://www.thingiverse.com/thing:5334329) with a BNC connector along with a 100pF capacitor. I used ~65' of 26awg BNTECHGO silicone wire which I directly soldered on (after tying a knot for strain relief). I also used a 2m piece of wire as dedicated counterpoise soldered to the ground tab of the BNC connector.

![Feather](/assets/Feather/feather2.webp)

## Tuning

I deployed it as a sloper per Chameleon's diagram above. Top of the wire was around 20' up on the DX Commander mast. I put it on an electric fence stake and fed it with 25' of RG-316 coax with a choke at the feed point. 

![Feather](/assets/Feather/feather3.webp)

For the tuning process, I got a slice of PEX pipe and drilled it to accommodate the antenna wire. This made it easy to trim the antenna wire to resonance and support the high end. This is a super lightweight end insulator.

![Feather](/assets/Feather/feather4.webp)

Results were looking pretty good but I noticed that the harmonics were starting to creep a bit. I got it pretty much OK for 40m (though it's still a bit long) and utilized a compensation coil to bring the upper bands back into resonance. The coil is installed about 6' from the transformer. For more info on the coil, [check out this video from Dude Tested](https://youtu.be/VvYtT-4UwqM?si=UrWQyweU6OysEnEi).

It's not too shabby. A bit high on the 40m band, but in my experience, it's always a bit schmutzy on 40m when it's deployed this low with a 49:1. I figured that I could always use my little manual tuner to touch up 40m if need be. 

## Conclusion

This is a nifty little build. I keep it in my QRP kit along with my [WarcWiz](https://jrschultz.github.io/VE5REV/posts/WARCWiz/) and my [20m QRP dipole](https://jrschultz.github.io/VE5REV/posts/20MQRP/). With these three antennas, I've got the 40m-10m bases covered for all my QRP needs.
